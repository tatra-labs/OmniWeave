# 17 — Risk Register and Open Questions

**In one paragraph.** This is the honest catalog of what could sink omniweave, kept as data rather
than prose: `tools/risks.toml` holds one row per risk with a likelihood, an impact, a **leading
indicator** wired to a real instrument, a mitigation already in the plan, a pre-committed
contingency, and one of **ten owner roles**. It carries the **kill criteria** — the specific,
measured observations under which a component or the project stops — the **assumptions** the plan
rests on with the consequence of each being false, and the consolidated open-questions list: the
charter's forty-one deferred items plus the **one hundred and sixty-two** raised by the other
eighteen plan documents, of which **fifty-two are recorded charter or cross-document defects
needing an ADR rather than an experiment**. It ends with the two lists that make an engineering plan
reviewable at all — what this plan deliberately over-engineers, and what it deliberately leaves
cheap.

Nothing here re-decides the charter, and nothing here re-decides a final plan document. Where a risk
is a settled decision's known cost, the row says so and names the pre-committed fallback verbatim
rather than inventing a second one.

## 1. The register is data, not prose

A risk register that lives in a document rots at the speed of the document. `tools/risks.toml` is
the machine-readable register; this document is its rendering plus the reasoning a TOML file cannot
carry.

```toml
# tools/risks.toml -- the machine-readable risk register. ONE KEY PER LINE.
[register]
horizon           = "v1.0"     # the window every `likelihood` is stated over
review_every_days = 90         # a row whose review_by exceeds reviewed_at by more than this fails
owner_roles       = [
  "licence", "model", "drivers", "router", "runtime",
  "retrieval", "out", "surface", "quality", "release",
]                              # byte-diffed against 17-risks.md section 1.2

[[risk]]
id          = "R-L1"
category    = "legal"
title       = "OpenRAIL-M weights on the highest-accuracy parsers"
likelihood  = 5                              # 1..5, see section 2
impact      = 5                              # 1..5, see section 2
owner       = "licence"
indicator   = "licence.tombstone_hits"       # a Counter id, a budget id, a gate id, or a codes.toml code
trigger     = "> 0 over a release"           # the observation that says it is materialising
mitigation  = "G3 three-way scan; shipped tombstones seeded with the weights licence_sha256"
contingency = "olmocr weights; the ~4-point olmOCR-bench gap is the price of clause 2(c)"
kill        = "K-11"                         # the kill criterion this risk can reach, or omitted
paths       = ["P2"]                         # the compound paths of section 4 this row belongs to
questions   = ["04#Q12"]                     # open-question keys (sections 7.3, 7.4)
reviewed_at = "2026-09-04"
review_by   = "2026-12-03"                   # reviewed_at + review_every_days
```

Five rules the checker enforces, mirroring how `eval/gates.toml` is treated:

1. **Every row's `indicator` resolves to a real instrument** — a `[[counter]]` id in
   `eval/counters.toml`, a `[[budget]]` id in `eval/perf.toml`, a `[[gate]]` id in `eval/gates.toml`,
   a `G<n>` or `Q-G<n>` row or a `[[job_assertion]]` name in `tools/gates.toml`, or a `Diag` or
   `Degradation` code in `codes.toml`. A row whose indicator is a sentence is a row nobody will ever
   notice materialising, and it fails.
2. **Every row with `likelihood * impact >= 12` carries a non-empty `contingency`.** Above that
   score, "we will think about it then" is not a plan.
3. **Every `questions` key resolves** to a row in section 7.2, 7.3 or 7.4 — so closing an open question
   mechanically lists the risks it retires.
4. **Every row carries an `owner` from the ten roles, a `reviewed_at` and a `review_by`**, and
   `review_by - reviewed_at <= review_every_days`. A row past `review_by` fails the build, exactly as
   `Q-G17` fails a gate row past its date. There is no non-blocking mode and no bypass flag: the
   override grammar is one named override per hazard, and there is no hazard here to name.
5. **Every `kill` value resolves to a criterion in section 5, and every criterion in section 5 is
   reached by at least one row.** A kill criterion nothing can reach is decoration; a risk whose
   worst case is a stop with no criterion is an unpriced exit.

### 1.1 Where the check lives, and why it is a job assertion rather than a gate

The reason is semantic, not arithmetic. Charter section 5 X32 fixed the CI gate set at `G1`-`G27`
"and nothing else"; **ADR-2 supersedes that** (erratum `E33`), allocates `G28` and `G29`, and makes
the series append-by-ADR with its cardinality in `tools/gates.toml`. The argument below is unchanged
by that and is the argument ADR-2 adopts for `gates.invariant_register` as well.
[Repository layout](11-repo-layout.md) section 8.6 spends `G27(d)` on `gate_docs.py` — the five
checks `d0`-`d4` over symbols, registers, links and cross-references — and its `d2` clause already
resolves every `tools/*.toml` filename, `G<n>`, `[[job_assertion]]` name and `INV-<n>` identifier a
document names. That is the correct home for *identifier resolution* and the wrong home for
*semantic assertions over a register's rows*.

[Repository layout](11-repo-layout.md) also ships the mechanism for exactly this case: an unnumbered
`[[job_assertion]]` row in `tools/gates.toml`, "a blocking CI check that belongs to one job rather
than to the G-series, because the G-series' cardinality is quoted in a core document and is therefore
append-only-by-ADR". **The register check is therefore `gates.risk_register`**, a
`[[job_assertion]]` in the `gates` job, budgeted at **2 s**: one `tomllib.load`, five set-membership
passes over 60 rows and ~200 question keys, and one byte-diff of section 1.2's table. It is not
`G27(e)`; there is no clause `e`.

Two consequences, both recorded rather than papered over. First,
[repository layout](11-repo-layout.md)'s Job-assertion definition enumerates **seven** rows at
release 1; with this one and with [principles](01-principles.md)'s `gate_invariants.py` — which
claims `G27(d)` and cannot have it, because `G27(d)` is already spent — the set is **nine**, the two
appends being `gates.risk_register` and `gates.invariant_register`. That is one row each in
`tools/gates.toml` plus a count correction in one lock row, and it is `RK-Q1`. Second, `Q-G17`'s rot
clock reads `eval/gates.toml`, `eval/counters.toml`, `eval/quarantine.toml` and `fixtures/index.toml`
([quality](13-quality.md) section 13.1); `tools/risks.toml` is deliberately **not** added to it,
because the register's rot clock is rule 4 of `gates.risk_register`, and one clock per register is
the point of not having two homes for one key.

### 1.2 The ten owner roles

A risk with no owner is a risk nobody is watching. The roles are deliberately few and map onto the
charter's ten decisions, not onto people, so the register survives a team change.

The **risks** column is *derived* — the register check regenerates it from `tools/risks.toml`'s
`owner` fields and byte-diffs it against this table, exactly as `ow surface emit --check` treats a
generated agent-facing artefact. A hand-edited cell fails `gates.risk_register`. The ids do **not**
partition by category prefix: `R-L4`, `R-L5` and `R-L9` are legal risks owned by `out` because the
decision that discharges them is a Target's, and `R-E6` and `R-E10` are owned by `licence` because
the decision is a vendoring one. **Category says what kind of failure it is; owner says whose
decision fixes it**, and conflating the two is how a register acquires rows nobody actually holds.

An owner role is **not** a staffing seat. [Roadmap](16-roadmap.md) section 3.1 allocates 363
engineer-weeks **by block A-I x phase** — one number per `(block, phase)` cell, summing both ways —
and not to seats at all. The seats `S-core`, `S-parse`, `S-rel`, `S-sec`, `S-quality` and `S-out`
are six of that document's nine section 15.2 skills, carried on each `W` work item rather than on
the matrix, and its decision-point table names leads. The `seat` column below maps each role onto
the skill that holds it, so the register does not become a third vocabulary for the same people.

**Sixty rows over ten roles**, and the distribution is itself a finding. Sorted, the loads are
2, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 9; the median is 5.5 and the two heaviest roles carry **1.6x** it.
`licence` and `release` tie at nine: every supply-chain, packaging, scope, schedule and
weights-licence risk lands in one of the two. If the ten roles ever map onto fewer than four people,
those two must be split before any other, and they must not be held by the same person — `release`
ships and `licence` refuses to ship, and one person holding both is the failure mode `R-A2`
describes. At the other end `router` holds two rows and `drivers` four; that is not evidence those
areas are safe, it is evidence about where this register's author has spent time, and `RK-Q2` says
so rather than presenting the distribution as a measurement.

## 2. The scales, stated as numbers

Adjectival risk registers are unfalsifiable. Both axes are defined against the **v1.0 horizon**
([roadmap](16-roadmap.md)'s week 64) and against consequences this plan already names.

**Likelihood** — the author's calibrated judgment that the risk materialises before v1.0 ships.
Where evidence exists it is cited in the row; where it does not, the row is a judgment and says so.

| L | band | reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | <= 5% | would surprise a reviewer who knows the collection |
| 2 | 5-20% | plausible; one measurement would settle it |
| 3 | 20-50% | expected in some form; the contingency will probably be read |
| 4 | 50-80% | expect it; plan the contingency as work, not as insurance |
| 5 | > 80% | already true, or certain absent a decision we have not taken |

**Impact** — measured in what has to change, not in feelings.

| I | consequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | a shipped **default** changes; one config key, one `Degradation` message, no schema and no document |
| 2 | one component reworked, <= 2 engineer-weeks; a plan document gains a section; no promise withdrawn |
| 3 | a plan document is **rewritten rather than defended**; a milestone slips >= 4 weeks; a published number is refit |
| 4 | a `CONTRACT` or `SCHEMA` major, a `digest_recipe` bump, or a **shipped promise withdrawn** — a card-visible downgrade, a determinism class lowered, a tier demoted |
| 5 | the thesis cannot be shipped: one of the seven claims in [vision](00-vision.md) section 3 becomes false and no invariant repairs it |

**Score** is `L * I`. Its only mechanical consequence is rule 2 of section 1: at `>= 12` a
contingency is mandatory. It is not a priority order — an `L1 * I5` legal risk outranks an `L4 * I2`
performance risk in every review that matters, and the register does not pretend arithmetic settles
that. Twenty of the sixty rows score `>= 12`; neither of the two rows the register gained for a
**closed** blocking defect (`R-T19`, `R-T20`) is among them, which is what closing one is supposed to
do to its likelihood.

## 3. The register

Category is the subheading. `L` and `I` are the section 2 scales. **Indicator** names the instrument
and the observation that says the risk is materialising; an id marked † does not yet exist and is
added to `eval/counters.toml` or `eval/perf.toml` by [quality](13-quality.md) (metrics) and
[observability](15-observability.md) (event fields) in the same change that merges this document.
**Mitigation** is what the plan already does; **contingency** is what happens when the mitigation
fails, and where a settled decision pre-committed one it is quoted rather than replaced. The `K`
column names the kill criterion the row can reach, and is empty where the row's worst case is a
degradation rather than an exit.

## 4. Compound paths: six combinations worse than their parts

A register of independent rows misses the failures that actually happen, which are conjunctions.
Each path below is a named conjunction, its joint likelihood — the product only where the events are
genuinely independent, otherwise argued — and the single decision that de-risks the whole path
earliest and most cheaply. `tools/risks.toml`'s `paths` array is the machine-readable membership, and
`gates.risk_register` asserts every `P<n>` named here has at least two member rows.

**P1 — F1 to the whole scale story.** `R-T2` (blocks/page off by 3-5x) forces `R-T12` (SQLite at T2)
and invalidates [architecture](02-architecture.md) sections 5.5 and 5.7,
[store](07-store-and-retrieval.md) section 4, `rss.gen5000p_peak_bytes`, the 8,192-block `Frame`
target and V20-2 simultaneously. The conjunction is not two risks, it is one measurement with nine
dependents. **Earliest cheapest decision:** run F1 first, before any storage number is quoted in a
document that ships — it is `fixtures/gen/gen_5000p_pdf.py` plus ten real 200-page documents, roughly
three engineer-days, and it retires or repoints nine claims at once. [Roadmap](16-roadmap.md) already
places it at DP8, the P2 exit in week 18, which is the earliest a measurement can run against a real
store; nothing in this register asks for it sooner, only that no storage number is *published*
before it.

**P2 — the permissive-weights monoculture.** `R-L1` is *managed* precisely by depending on olmocr,
and `R-E3` says olmocr is 88% one author and 5.2 months quiet with Apache-2.0 weights that nothing
in the plan can reproduce if their host withdraws them. The mitigation for the legal risk **is** the
supply risk: there is exactly one permissive high-accuracy OCR family in the collection, and if it
goes, the `PAGE` rung has no producer and scans leave the shipped scope (`K-11`). Joint likelihood is
not a product — the two are anti-correlated in blame and correlated in effect. **Earliest cheapest
decision:** mirror the weights by digest into the same ZIP + `manifest.toml` + `.sha256` channel
`omniweave-golden` already uses, installed by digest into `$OMNIWEAVE_HOME/` and re-verified on
every use, so upstream withdrawal costs a mirror and not a capability. That mechanism exists; it
just is not pointed at weights yet. `11#OQ-1`'s zstd caveat is discharged rather than inherited:
ADR-10 D1 makes both data artefacts a plain ZIP with `ZIP_DEFLATED` members
(`<name>-<version>.zip` + `manifest.toml` + `<name>-<version>.zip.sha256`), and D6 rules this mirror
a third artefact of that family in that container, read by `omniweave-vision`. Its name and refresh
cadence remain this path's own decision.

**P3 — the graph is expensive and empty.** `R-T10` (median `covered_frac` < 0.2) and `R-T9`
(quarantine rate > 30%) are the two halves of the same audit and, together, mean L3 spends money to
produce a graph with poor coverage and a high true-fact rejection rate. Either alone is a retune;
both is `K-8` — L3 ships as Segments and anchors only, and entities, edges, claims and communities
leave release 1. **Earliest cheapest decision:** F13 and F12 are the *same* 500-Segment labelled
audit over the *same* seven document classes. Run them as one exercise (~10 engineer-days including
labelling) before the entity resolution funnel, the nine guards, the `[75,92)` band and
`community_report` are built — because building them and then cutting them is the single largest
avoidable waste in the plan. [Roadmap](16-roadmap.md) DP6 sets week 46, "before P8's billed passes",
which is the right anchor; the risk this path adds is that the labelling is the long pole and 10 ed
of labelling does not compress under schedule pressure.

**P4 — the OUT abstraction and the empty tier.** `R-T6` (the contract fits none of the three) and
`R-E8` (no licence-clean rasterizer, so `bake` has zero live rows) compound: an abstraction that is
already straining is being asked to carry a `Disposition` member nothing implements. Every unused
degree of freedom in `constructs.toml` is a place where the three targets can disagree without
anyone noticing. **Earliest cheapest decision:** resolve `bake` **before** the third target's
ConstructTable is written — `K-5` demotes it from `unavailable` to `forbidden`, deleting one
`Disposition` member, one gate branch and one refusal path from all three tables at once. DP5 sets
week 40, before P9 starts, which is before the first ConstructTable rather than the third; that is
stricter than this path needs and is the right side to err on. `R-T18` sits on the same path in the
other direction: cutting `deck` under `K-10` removes the only target whose measurement rules the
Python side cannot recompute, which shrinks `R-T6`'s abstraction problem from three carriers to two.

**P5 — the incumbent gets better while we are still building.** `R-E2` (context windows grow) and
`R-A3` (`source_reread_rate` stays high) are the same failure observed from two ends: the agent does
not need us, and the agent does not trust us. The 30-task harness measures both, against the same
scripted agent, in the same run, under [quality](13-quality.md)'s `Control guard`. **Earliest
cheapest decision:** build the harness **with its no-omniweave control arm** at v0.1 rather than at
v1.0. It is the instrument for F19, `source_reread_rate`, `generated_share`, `task_accuracy` and
`R-E2`'s trigger — five of the plan's most load-bearing numbers come out of one ~30-task fixture set
at 8 engineer-days, and [roadmap](16-roadmap.md) schedules F19 at DP11, the P6 exit in week 40, which
is after P7 builds the surface the harness is supposed to justify. This register's one scheduling
disagreement with the roadmap is this row, and `RK-Q8` states it as a question rather than a change.

**P6 — the buffer, the backlog and the ADRs, all in front of v0.1.** `R-A9` (20.3 ew of measurement
against a 6 ew buffer), `R-O9` (fifty-two recorded defects against zero ADRs) and `R-A1` (scope) are
one path, not three, because they all draw on the same unallocated time. The arithmetic:
**27 engineer-days of measurement is anchored at v0.1 or at an ordering condition** — F1, F2, F4, F8
and F19 at v0.1 (17 ed) plus F9, F10, F14, F16 and F31 as ordering conditions naming no tier (10 ed)
— and section 7.6's twelve ADRs are unpriced but gate `G27(c)`, `ow doctor` passing on a fresh
install, and `Q-G6`'s normaliser chain being well-defined. None of that is in the 363 ew.
**Earliest cheapest decision:** price and place ADR-1 and ADR-3 before P1 exits. They are the two
that unblock other work — ADR-1 is one mechanical lock pass plus one check fix, ADR-3 is a set of
missing rows in shipped files — and both are engineer-days, not engineer-weeks. Deferring them does
not save the time; it moves the cost into every document that has to say "as printed, this fails its
own check".

## 5. Kill criteria

A plan without kill criteria is not honest, because every mechanism in it can be defended
indefinitely by someone who likes it. Each criterion below is an **observation**, not a judgment:
an instrument, a threshold, and what specifically stops. Three project-level, ten component-level.

A kill criterion is discharged by a recorded decision, never by silence. `ow eval release-check`
refuses on `unknown`, and the same rule applies here: a criterion whose instrument has not run is
`unknown`, and `unknown` is not "not triggered". The **reached by** column is the inverse of the
register's `kill` field, and `gates.risk_register` byte-diffs the two directions against each other,
so a criterion nothing can reach fails the build.

  `semantic: off`. A visible product limitation and the correct one; the alternative is a five-second
  query or numpy in core.
- **No confidence signal is calibrated at v1.0.** Then nothing renders a digit — INV-19 already
  requires that, and the internal-filter clause keeps `Trust` and `Quote` usable as sort keys. The
  mechanism is designed to be *earned into*, which is V20-5, not removed.
- **`verbatim` is unreachable on the office path at release 1.** anydoc's card honestly declares
  `origin_span = "none"` because `anydoc/src/model/block.rs` carries no source address of any kind;
  `corpus_card.verbatim_fraction` discloses the consequence before any query runs, and the fork
  trigger's first named clause is the fix. What **is** a defect, and is `R-T16`, is that the two
  gates measuring fidelity both pass anyway.
- **HTML, email, audio and video are out of the shipped input surface.** V01-17 fails mechanically if
  a format token has no producer, so the mismatch between charter section 1's own first sentence and
  the shipped set is a gate failure rather than a quiet overclaim. Time-based media additionally
  needs a time address `Locus` cannot express, which makes it a model change and not a Driver.

And one meta-criterion for this register itself: **a risk row whose `indicator` has no instrument by
the milestone that first exposes the risk is a failed row**, not a watched one. It fails
`gates.risk_register`, which is the only way a register avoids becoming a document nobody reads.

### 7.1 Two conventions, stated once

**Deadlines are anchored, not dated.** Each row's deadline is one of the tiers in
[vision](00-vision.md) section 9 (`v0.1`, `v1.0`, `v2.0`) or a charter-named ordering condition
("before the golden suite freezes", "before the vector phase starts", "before anything ships").
[Roadmap](16-roadmap.md)'s decision-point table owns the mapping from those anchors to calendar
weeks and to a named lead, and its `Decision point` concept is stronger than a deadline: a dated
obligation whose **pre-committed action executes automatically if the date passes unmeasured**. This
document owns the anchors, because an anchor survives a schedule change and a date does not —
`A17` is why that matters rather than being pedantry.

**Cost is engineer-days (`ed`) plus machine cost where it is not zero.** A cost with a machine
figure names the instrument; GPU figures assume an L40S at spot pricing. These are the same p50
estimates [roadmap](16-roadmap.md) declares its own numbers to be, and `R-A9` records that the two
documents' totals differ by 1.65x.

**Class** is one of **defect** (a binding document contradicts itself, another document, or the lock;
needs an ADR — see section 7.6), **gap** (the charter is silent where a document needed an answer;
needs a decision), **measurement** (a number closes it), or **decision** (a choice with no
measurement to make it).

### 7.7 The `F`-number allocation, so this never collides again

[Store and retrieval](07-store-and-retrieval.md) coined `F50`-`F55` and
[interfaces](10-interfaces.md) coined `F60`-`F65`, both continuing the charter's series from a
document. That instinct is right and the mechanism is wrong, because two documents written in
parallel would both have taken `F50` — and two documents did in fact take adjacent blocks by luck
rather than by rule. **Extends the charter:** `F` numbers are allocated in per-document blocks, and a
document may only coin inside its own block.

| block | owner | used |

| `F1`-`F41` | the charter | closed |

Only 07 and 10 ever used the series. Every other document kept a local scheme — `V-Q`, plain
numbers, `Q`, `N`/`T`, `OQ`, `P-Q`, `Q-Q`, `R-Q`, `RK-Q` — and those ids are already referenced by
name in shipped text, so nothing is renamed. **The `<doc>#<id>` key of sections 7.3 and 7.4 is the
addressing scheme that actually works**, and the block table above exists to keep the two documents
that did coin `F` numbers from colliding with a future amendment, not to encourage more of them. A
new document should use a local prefix, not a block.

One collision to note explicitly, because `G27(d)`'s `d2` clause resolves identifiers by pattern:
[roadmap](16-roadmap.md)'s open questions are `R-Q1`-`R-Q7` and this register's rows are
`R-T<n>`, `R-E<n>`, `R-L<n>`, `R-O<n>` and `R-A<n>`. **There is no risk category `Q`**, and there
never will be; `R-Q` is unambiguously the roadmap's. The register's id grammar is
`R-[TELOA][0-9]+`, and `gates.risk_register` enforces it.

## 10. Operating rhythm

The register is worthless without a cadence, and a cadence is worthless without a refusal attached.

| when | what | the refusal |
|---|---|---|
| Every PR | `gates.risk_register` runs in the `gates` job inside a 2 s budget: every `indicator` resolves to an instrument, every `questions` key resolves to a section 7.2/7.3/7.4 row, every `kill` resolves to a section 5 criterion and every criterion is reached, every row scoring `>= 12` has a contingency, every row has an owner from the ten roles and a `review_by` no more than 90 days past its `reviewed_at`, and section 1.2's derived table byte-matches the register | The PR fails. There is no non-blocking mode and no bypass flag |
| Every release | `ow eval release-check` refuses on `unknown`. **Extends the charter:** it additionally refuses when any `tools/risks.toml` row is past `review_by`, and when any **kill criterion's instrument has not run** — an unmeasured criterion is `unknown`, and `unknown` is not "not triggered" | The release does not tag |
| Every 90 days | The ten owner roles review their rows: likelihood, impact, indicator, and whether a closed open question retires the row. A row reviewed and unchanged still gets a new `reviewed_at` and `review_by`, recorded, with a name against it | Rule 4 fails the build at day 91 |
| Every decision-point week | [Roadmap](16-roadmap.md)'s decision-point table fires: the pre-committed action of any item whose date passes **unmeasured** executes automatically. This register does not duplicate those dates; it supplies the anchors the table maps and the register row each item retires | The gate-rot check fails the build on a gate row past its `review_by` |
| Every tier boundary (v0.1 → v1.0 → v2.0) | Every open question whose deadline anchor is that tier is closed with a measurement, or **explicitly re-deferred with a new closing measurement and a new anchor** — which is V20-8, generalised from the charter's forty-one to all 186 distinct items | `Q-G17` already fails a gate row past its date; the same rule applies here |

Three rules about the register itself, so it does not become the thing nobody reads:

1. **A risk is retired by a closed question or a shipped mechanism, never by time passing.** A row
   whose likelihood drops with no cited evidence is a row someone got tired of.
2. **A new risk needs an indicator before it needs a mitigation.** An unmeasurable risk is an
   anxiety, and the register is not for anxieties.
3. **A kill criterion may be revised only upward in strictness, or by an ADR that names the old
   threshold and the new one.** Loosening a kill criterion quietly is how a project with kill
   criteria becomes a project without them.

## Open questions

Numbered `RK-Q` so downstream documents can reference them. Charter `F1`-`F41` and sections 7.3 and
7.4's rows are not restated; only questions this document's own subject raises.

1. **RK-Q1 — answered by ADR-2: the `G27(d)` collision, and the job-assertion count.**
   [Principles](01-principles.md) open question 3 claimed `G27(d)` for `tools/gate_invariants.py`;
   [repository layout](11-repo-layout.md) section 8.6 already spends `G27(d)` on `gate_docs.py` and
   specifies its five sub-checks. Both documents were final, and both read charter section 5 X32 as
   forbidding a twenty-eighth `G` gate; they collided because they were written in parallel. This
   document resolved it in 11's favour and took the mechanism 11 itself supplies: the register check
   is the `[[job_assertion]]` row `gates.risk_register`, and 01's checker becomes
   `gates.invariant_register`. **ADR-2 confirms both**, on the semantic ground of section 1.1 rather
   than on X32 — it supersedes X32's closure, allocates `G28` and `G29`, and still declines to make
   either register check a gate, because a register's semantics are not a document check. **What
   that changed:** 11's Job-assertion lock row enumerates seven rows at release 1 and the correct
   number is **nine**, the two appends being `gates.risk_register` and `gates.invariant_register`.
   That count correction is the one edit this question leaves open, and it is
   [11](11-repo-layout.md)'s.

2. **RK-Q2 — are likelihood judgments worth stating at all?** Every `L` in section 3 is a calibrated
   judgment, not a measurement; nothing in the collection reports base rates for "a document model
   was wrong late" or "an ANN library failed a licence scan". The register's mechanical rules key off
   `L * I >= 12`, so a mis-set `L` changes whether a contingency is mandatory, and section 1.2's own
   load distribution is evidence that the author's attention is unevenly spread rather than that the
   risks are. **Closes:** at v1.0, score each row against what actually happened and report the
   Brier score of the whole register — the same discipline `Q-G12` applies to a confidence signal.
   **Pre-committed fallback:** if the register's own Brier score is worse than a constant-0.5
   predictor, drop `L` entirely and make the contingency requirement key off `I >= 3` alone, which
   needs no forecast.

3. **RK-Q3 — the kill criteria have no named decider.** Each criterion names an instrument, a
   threshold and what stops; none names who is authorised to declare it met. The ten owner roles own
   *mitigation*, and a role that owns a mechanism is the worst-placed party to declare that mechanism
   dead. [Roadmap](16-roadmap.md)'s decision-point table names a lead per row and "The program" for
   the two rows nobody inside a component can decide, which is the shape the answer should take.
   **Closes:** a governance ADR naming the decider for a component-level criterion — plausibly the
   `release` role, whose interests are orthogonal — and for a project-level one, which cannot be a
   role inside the project at all. This is a real gap and it is the one most likely to be quietly
   ignored, because the criteria that matter most are the ones nobody inside wants to declare.

4. **RK-Q4 — the control arm is specified as a comparison and not as a configuration.**
   [Quality](13-quality.md) specifies the harness half properly: a `Control guard` whose baseline is
   the paired no-omniweave arm of the *same* nightly run, `task_accuracy` at paired `p < 0.01`,
   `source_reread_rate` at 1.0 on the control arm by construction, every model call through a
   Cassette including the scripted agent's, and results reported as a distribution sliced by
   born-digital versus scanned. What is **not** specified anywhere is the control arm's own
   toolset: which host tools it has (`Read`, `Grep`, a shell), which converter it uses per format,
   and its stopping rule. That is not a detail — "whatever the host happens to use" is exactly the
   variable [vision](00-vision.md) section 1.1 blames for the five losses, and a control arm using a
   *bad* converter rigs `R-E2`'s trigger in our favour. **Closes:** [quality](13-quality.md) naming
   the control arm's converter per format and its stopping rule, with the bias recorded: **use the
   best available converter for each format**, because a comparison we can only win against a straw
   man tells us nothing about `R-E2`. **Closed by [ADR-14](adr/0014-reference-corpora-and-control-arm.md) D14.3:** the same agent with the server removed, `Read`/`Grep`/read-only `Bash`, pypdfium2 and anydoc for both arms with a page image where a PDF page has no text layer, and a 12-call budget ending on a turn with no tool call.

5. **RK-Q5 — a closed literal that eight documents need to extend is closed at the wrong place.**
   `Degradation.kind` is declared closed and extensible only by D6, D9 and D10 (the charter's D4
   block, section 5 X21). [Observability](15-observability.md) shows it is short by **eleven**
   members and that all eleven are already constructed by written plan documents —
   `discovery_slow`, `no_driver`, `store_busy`, `rung_ceiling`, `stat_stale`,
   `pushdown_unavailable`, `vector_scan_over_budget`, `cache_disabled_no_space`,
   `compile_normalized`, `scope_clamped` — with `events_dropped` added under 15's own D10 grant.
   One of the ten is direct evidence for this row's conclusion: `compile_normalized` is constructed
   by [generation](09-generation.md) section 5.3, and 09 section 15 justifies it as "extending the
   existing `kind` literal exactly as D6, D9 and D10 do" — but 09 is **D8**, which X21 does not
   name, so the sentence asserts an authority the charter does not grant. This document needs a
   twelfth for a *removed* disposition: `K-5` and `R-E8` describe a documented degradation for a
   `Disposition` member that no longer exists, and there is no member for that either. Following the
   charter, nothing is invented here. **Closes:** ADR-3 takes the literal to twenty-seven members,
   and decides separately whether `Disposition` removal is a release note rather than a runtime
   degradation — the honest answer is probably a release note, because a degradation names something
   that happened during a run and a vocabulary removal does not. Recorded because a closed literal
   that eight parallel documents — 02, 04, 05, 07, 08, 09, 10 and 15 — all need to extend is a sign
   the closure is drawn at the wrong place, not that eight authors were careless.

6. **RK-Q6 — no cost has been assigned to the ADR backlog.** Section 7.6's twelve ADRs are the
   prerequisite for `G27(c)`, for `ow doctor` passing on a fresh install, for `Q-G6`'s normaliser
   chain being well-defined, and for three signatures [api sketch](18-api-sketch.md) could not
   write — which puts them on the critical path of v0.1 rather than beside it (path **P6**). This
   document sized every open question in engineer-days and did not size these, and
   [roadmap](16-roadmap.md) has no ADR line at all: its exclusions cover documentation, recruiting
   and the F-item measurement runs, and are silent on the ADRs.
   **Closes:** [roadmap](16-roadmap.md) pricing ADR-1 through ADR-12 and placing them, with my bias
   recorded: ADR-1 and ADR-3 unblock other work and belong before the P1 exit, ADR-5 is the one
   whose outcome could change a shipped type, and ADR-9 is the cheapest thing in the plan that
   discharges a stated rejection criterion.

7. **RK-Q7 — two final documents price the same measurement backlog 1.65x apart.** Section 7.2
   itemises the forty-one deferred items at 101.5 engineer-days (20.3 ew);
   [roadmap](16-roadmap.md)'s exclusion 7 prices them at "~14 additional bench-weeks … plus roughly
   0.3 ew of analysis each", which is 12.3 ew. The 8 ew gap sits against a **6 ew program buffer**,
   and it is concentrated in four items a uniform per-item average cannot see: F12+F13 at 10 ed
   (labelling-bound), F17 at 8 ed and F19 at 8 ed. Both documents declare their numbers p50
   estimates, so this is not a contradiction in kind, only in magnitude — but the magnitude is
   larger than the buffer. **Closes:** one sentence in exclusion 7 replacing the uniform 0.3 ew with
   section 7.2's itemisation, after which `R-A9`'s indicator has something to compare against. The
   register's own position, stated so nobody has to infer it: **at a 1.6% buffer the expected
   outcome is that several deferred items ship on their pre-committed fallback rather than on a
   measurement**, and that is survivable precisely because every one of them has a fallback written
   down.

8. **RK-Q8 — F19 is scheduled after the surface it exists to justify.** The 30-task
   `bench/serve/` harness with its control arm is the instrument for F19, `source_reread_rate`,
   `generated_share`, `task_accuracy` and `R-E2`'s trigger — five of the plan's most load-bearing
   numbers out of one 8 ed fixture set — and `K-1`, the project-level kill criterion, reads two of
   them. [Roadmap](16-roadmap.md) places it at DP11, the P6 exit in week 40, which is after P7
   builds the agent surface. This register's position is that the harness belongs at v0.1, before
   the surface, because an instrument built after the thing it measures tends to measure what was
   built. **Closes:** a decision at the P6 planning point, owned by the program, informed by
   `16#R-Q2` (whether v0.1 ships publicly at all, since F19 needs real MCP hosts). This is the one
   place where this document disagrees with a final plan document about sequencing, and it is
   recorded as a question rather than taken as a change.

| `tools/risks.toml` | file | The machine-readable risk register: one `[[risk]]` per row carrying `id`, `category`, `title`, `likelihood`, `impact`, `owner`, `indicator`, `trigger`, `mitigation`, `contingency`, an optional `kill`, a `paths` array, a `questions` array, `reviewed_at` and `review_by`.
